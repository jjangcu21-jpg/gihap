865. The driver of an electric kick scooter is trying to turn left. Which of the following are the safe ways to do so in the given situation? (Select TWO)

  1. ①Use Crosswalk A while riding the electric kick scooter, wait at Point B for the traffic light to turn green, and go in Direction D.
  2. ②Use Crosswalk A while pushing the electric kick scooter, wait at Point B for the traffic light to turn green, and go in Direction D.
  3. ③Ride the electric kick scooter, turn left toward the center of the intersection, and go in Direction E.
  4. ④Ride the electric kick scooter going straight to Point B, wait for the traffic light to turn green, and go in Direction D.
  5. ⑤Ride the electric kick scooter going straight to Point C and immediately go to Point B to go straight in Direction D.
Show answer

Answer
② Use Crosswalk A while pushing the electric kick scooter, wait at Point B for the traffic light to turn green, and go in Direction D.
④ Ride the electric kick scooter going straight to Point B, wait for the traffic light to turn green, and go in Direction D.

Reference
Article 25, Paragraph 3 of the Road Traffic Act. When drivers of bicycles, etc. wish to turn left at an intersection, they must first move to the right edge of the road, drive slowly, and turn left using the edge of the intersection. Ministry of Government Legislation law interpretation case. Agenda No. 17-0078. (The bicycles below are currently classified as bicycles, etc.) According to Article 25 (2) of the Road Traffic Act, when drivers of all vehicles intend to turn left at an intersection, they must drive slowly along the center line of the road in advance and turn left using the inside of the center of the intersection. However, drivers may pass outside the center of the intersection at locations specifically designated as necessary by the city/provincial police chief (proviso), and Paragraph 3 of the same Article stipulates that, notwithstanding Paragraph 2 of the same Article, if a bicycle driver intends to turn left at an intersection, he/she must stick to the right edge of the road in advance, drive slowly, and turn left using the edge of the intersection. This stipulates how cyclists should turn left at an intersection. Article 25 (3) of the Road Traffic Act states that cyclists cannot immediately turn left at an intersection according to a left turn signal, and must stay on the right edge of the road according to a signal to go straight in the direction of travel in two successive straight-ahead stages. Does this mean a so-called “hook-turn,” which means turning left in a “straight-go-straight” manner, or does it mean that bicycle drivers can turn left immediately following a left turn signal at an intersection, but must turn left using the part of the road on the driver’s right, such as a car turning left in the same direction pursuant to Paragraph 2 of the same article? Article 25, Paragraph 3 of the Road Traffic Act stipulates a “hook turn,” which involves turning left in the manner of “going straight in two steps - going straight” according to the signal for going straight in the direction of travel, and it has been interpreted as a violation of the regulation if a cyclist, like a car driver, turns left immediately at a green left turn arrow signal, etc. However, the civil petitioner has a disagreement with the above interpretation of the National Police Agency and requested the Ministry of Government Legislation to interpret the law. 2. Conclusion. Article 25, Paragraph 3 of the Road Traffic Act, which stipulates how cyclists, etc., can turn left at intersections, refers to a “hook turn,” which means that cyclists cannot immediately turn left according to a left turn signal at an intersection, but must turn left in a “two-step straight-go” method by sticking to the right edge of the road according to a signal to go straight in the direction of travel.
